## Abstract

Unmanned aerial vehicles could accurately accomplish complex navigation and obstacle avoidance tasks under external control. However, enabling unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to rely solely on onboard computation and sensing for real-time navigation and dynamic obstacle avoidance remains a significant challenge due to stringent latency and energy constraints. In our previous work, we introduced a fully neuromorphic framework for dynamic obstacle avoidance on tiny quadrotors. While it achieved low-latency avoidance, its reliance on a single monocular event camera and visual homing caused redundant avoidance of distant obstacles and prevented autonomous navigation in unknown environments. In this work, inspired by the efficiency of biological systems, we propose an odometry-free fully autonomous neuromorphic framework achieving real-time end-to-end obstacle avoidance and navigation in unknown signal-denied dynamic environment with an overall latency of just 2.54 milliseconds. Specifically, our bio-inspired approach enables accurate moving objects detection and avoidance in complex dynamic environment without requiring target recognition or trajectory computation during navigation and exploration process. We validate our system on an autonomous quadrotor using only onboard resources, demonstrating reliable navigation and avoidance of diverse obstacles moving at speeds up to 15 m/s under different light conditions, with energy consumption reduced to 20.38% compared to traditional architecture.
